The Uniqueness of CTOPP-2

While most tests measure what a child *reads*, the **CTOPP-2** measures the **underlying cognitive machinery** needed for reading. It identifies hidden weaknesses in how the brain processes the sounds of language, which is often the "root cause" of dyslexia.

Rapid Naming (RAN)

Predicts reading fluency by measuring how fast the brain retrieves verbal labels.

Auditory Memory

Tests the capacity to hold speech sounds in mind long enough to blend them.

Sound Manipulation

Assesses the ability to add, delete, or swap sounds within spoken words.

Core Composite Analysis

Phonological Awareness (PA)

This is the ability to perceive and manipulate sounds. Leo's score of **62** indicates severe difficulty with tasks like "Elision" (saying 'cup' without the 'k' sound). This is a foundational deficit for reading.

Rapid Symbolic Naming (RSN)

Leo's score of **68** shows that his brain takes significantly longer to "pull" verbal names for numbers and letters from memory. Even if he learns the sounds, he will likely struggle with reading speed and fluency.

Phonological Memory (PM)

At **82**, this is a relative area of higher performance but still borderline. He can hold some sounds in mind, but multi-syllabic words will cause a "memory overflow," leading to errors at the end of long words.

What this means at home

When Leo "forgets" a word he just read on the previous page, it isn't a memory problem in the traditional sense. It's his **Phonological Processing Engine** struggling to retrieve the data fast enough.

Patience with repetitive reading is key.
