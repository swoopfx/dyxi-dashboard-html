Dyslexia Type Evaluation

Detailed breakdown of specific dyslexia subtypes for Leo Jenkins.

Leo Jenkins Maya Jenkins

1. Phonological Dyslexia

(Dysphonetic or Auditory Dyslexia)

88%
Probability Level

Detailed Explanation

Phonological dyslexia is characterized by a significant difficulty in the ability to manipulate the individual sounds of language. Individuals struggle to match letters to their corresponding sounds (grapheme-phoneme mapping), making it nearly impossible to "sound out" or decode unfamiliar or nonsense words.

Reason for Decision

Leo's assessment shows a critical deficit in phonological awareness. He consistently fails to blend sounds in CVC words and relies heavily on visual memorization. During the "nonsense word" test, he was unable to decode 90% of the targets, instead attempting to substitute them with visually similar real words.

Indicator Interpretation: The high probability suggests that Leo's primary reading barrier is auditory-based processing of language sounds, requiring intensive multisensory phonics intervention.

2. Surface Dyslexia

(Dyseidetic or Visual Dyslexia)

45%
Probability Level

Detailed Explanation

Surface dyslexia involves difficulty recognizing words as whole units (sight words). While the individual can often decode regular words using phonics, they struggle with "exception words" that do not follow standard spelling rules (e.g., "island," "enough").

Reason for Decision

Leo shows a moderate ability to recognize common high-frequency words. However, his accuracy drops significantly when presented with irregular spellings. He often attempts to "over-regularize" these words (e.g., pronouncing the 's' in 'island'). This suggests his visual word form area is functioning but lacks the robust orthographic mapping needed for fluency.

Indicator Interpretation: Leo's sight-word vocabulary is developing but fragile. He requires specific orthographic training to build a reliable "mental dictionary."

3. Rapid-Naming Dyslexia

(Rapid Automatic Naming Deficit)

72%
Probability Level

Detailed Explanation

This type refers to the inability to quickly name familiar symbols like letters, numbers, or colors. It is a deficit in retrieval speed rather than knowledge. It significantly impacts reading fluency because the brain cannot process visual symbols fast enough to keep up with the demands of continuous text.

Reason for Decision

Leo's RAN (Rapid Automated Naming) scores are in the 15th percentile. While he knows his letters and colors perfectly, his time to name a sequence of them is twice the expected duration for his age. This "bottleneck" in processing speed is a major contributor to his slow reading rate and high frustration levels.

Indicator Interpretation: The high probability indicates that even if Leo learns to decode, his reading will likely remain slow and labored without specific fluency-building exercises.

4. Double-Deficit Dyslexia

(Combined Phonological and RAN Deficit)

92%
Probability Level

Detailed Explanation

Double-deficit dyslexia occurs when an individual has both a phonological awareness deficit and a rapid naming deficit. This is considered the most severe form of dyslexia, as it impacts both the accuracy of decoding and the speed of processing.

Reason for Decision

Because Leo scores extremely low in both phonological processing (88% prob) and rapid naming (72% prob), he fits the clinical profile for double-deficit dyslexia. His reading struggles are multifaceted, involving both the "how" (decoding) and the "how fast" (fluency), leading to a compounded impact on overall literacy development.

Indicator Interpretation: This is the most critical finding. Leo requires a highly structured, intensive, and comprehensive intervention program that addresses both decoding accuracy and processing speed simultaneously.

5. Visual Dyslexia

(Specific Visual Processing Issues)

32%
Probability Level

Detailed Explanation

Visual dyslexia involves difficulties with visual processing that affect reading, such as letters appearing to move, blur, or reverse. It is distinct from vision problems (eyesight) and relates to how the brain interprets visual information on the page.

Reason for Decision

Leo exhibits persistent letter reversal patterns, particularly with 'b/d' and 'p/q' confusions that occur even in high-frequency words. Tracing analysis reveals specific difficulties with visual tracking; his gaze often skips lines or loses its place when transitioning between sentences. Additionally, he shows signs of visual crowding, where letters appear to 'clump' together during sustained reading tasks. While these issues contribute to the 32% probability, they are currently viewed as secondary to his primary phonological deficits.

Indicator Interpretation: Visual processing is a secondary concern. While dyslexia-friendly fonts and clear layouts will help, the focus should remain on phonological and fluency interventions.
