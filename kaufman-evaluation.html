How to Read This Report

Standard Score (SS)

Think of this like an IQ score. **100 is average.** Scores between 85 and 115 are considered within the normal range. Below 80 suggests a significant struggle.

Discrepancy

This is the "gap" between how smart a child is and how well they read. A large gap is a classic sign of dyslexia.

Index Level

The "Index" combines multiple tests into one number to show the overall likelihood of dyslexia.

KTEA-3 Detailed Subtest Profile

A look at the specific building blocks of Leo's reading.

Phonological Processing (Score: 70) Severe Deficit

**Plain English:** The ability to hear and play with individual sounds in words.

**Classroom Impact:** Leo might struggle to rhyme, or have trouble knowing that if you take the "m" off "mat," you get "at." He finds it very hard to "map" a sound to a letter.

Nonsense Word Decoding (Score: 72) Severe Deficit

**Plain English:** Reading made-up words like "flim" or "zop."

**Classroom Impact:** This test is crucial because Leo can't "guess" a nonsense word from context. It shows he is truly struggling to sound out or decode words he hasn't seen before.

Word Recognition (Score: 78) Weak

**Plain English:** Recognizing "sight words" or words he has seen before.

**Classroom Impact:** Leo is trying to memorize what words look like as pictures, rather than reading them. This works for simple words but fails as words get harder.

Reading Comprehension (Score: 88) Relative Strength

**Plain English:** Understanding the meaning of what is read.

**Classroom Impact:** This is Leo's superpower. He is smart enough to "figure out" what a story is about even if he can't read all the words. He is using context and vocabulary to compensate for reading difficulties.

Glossary of Terms

Phoneme

The smallest unit of sound in speech (like the 'b' in 'bat').

Grapheme

The written letter or letters that represent a sound.

Orthographic Mapping

How the brain stores words permanently for instant retrieval.

Decoding

The process of translating written words into speech (sounding out).

The Big Picture

Leo is a very bright child who is "working around" his reading problems. He is using his high intelligence to guess words and understand context.

**The Problem:** He hasn't mastered the basic "sound code" of English.

**The Solution:** He needs "Structured Literacy" or "Orton-Gillingham" style tutoring that explicitly teaches him how sounds and letters work together.

Specific Challenge Areas

Blending Sounds

Difficulty taking "c-a-t" and putting it together to say "cat."

Visual Word Storage

Trouble remembering what words look like once they get longer than 4 letters.

Processing Fatigue

He gets tired very quickly when reading because he is working twice as hard as his peers.

Recommended Next Steps

  • Discuss this report with Leo's teacher to arrange support.
  • Look for a "Structured Literacy" tutor in your area.
  • Use audiobooks to help Leo keep up with stories he loves.
